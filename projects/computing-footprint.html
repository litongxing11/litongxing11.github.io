FORECASTING & ENVIRONMENTAL MODELING

The environmental cost of high-performance computing.

I led a two-person modeling study of how data centers connect computing demand to electricity consumption, carbon emissions, and cooling-water use.

When
Grade 10 · 2024
Focus
Environmental modeling
My role
Two-person team leader

The question

As demand for computing grows, how might its environmental footprint change? We examined electricity demand, the mix of energy sources supplying it, and water consumption to explore where efficiency improvements could make a difference.

How we modeled it

  • Compare forecasts. We compared AR, MA, ARMA, ARIMA, SARIMA, and Holt-Winters models for electricity-demand and operational-efficiency trends, reporting mean squared error and mean absolute error alongside their projections.
  • Project the electricity mix. Using generation data from 1985–2023, we searched ARIMA orders and trend specifications with the Akaike information criterion (AIC), then forecast fossil-fuel generation and renewables plus nuclear power through 2030.
  • Explore emissions. A Gaussian generalized linear model related carbon emissions to electricity consumption, generation by source, lagged consumption, and an interaction between fossil-fuel and renewable generation.
  • Extend to water use. We compared time-series projections using historical water-use data from 2012–2021, examining how model choice changed the outlook for cooling demand.

What the models showed

Our electricity-mix forecast put renewables plus nuclear power at approximately 45% of global generation by 2030. This was a projection under the fitted historical trends.

Forecasts differed substantially across models. For water use, AR projected rapid growth, ARIMA and SARIMA suggested more moderate increases, and other models produced flatter or declining paths. That spread made model selection central to interpreting the results.

The emissions regression also had limits: its non-intercept coefficients did not reach the 5% significance threshold. We treated these estimates as exploratory associations, rather than evidence of causal effects.

From models to recommendations

We proposed improving cooling efficiency and server utilization, adopting hardware suited to specific workloads, and using energy-aware scheduling. We also considered cleaner electricity supplies and recovering server waste heat for nearby buildings or industrial uses.

The project reinforced the value of considering electricity, emissions, and water together when evaluating computing infrastructure.

My role & output

I led the two-person team. Our 25-page report brought together model comparisons, electricity-mix forecasts, emissions analysis, water-use projections, and practical recommendations.

Methods

Time-series forecasting · ARIMA & SARIMA · Holt-Winters · AIC model selection · Gaussian GLM · Forecast error comparison
